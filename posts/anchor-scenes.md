### Anchor Scenes

1. **Initial Friendship and Innocence**
   - Playing video games together and watching South Park after the family is asleep.
   - Feeling comfortable and trusted as friends.

2. **Skipping School & Bonding**
   - The first day of skipping school, hiding in the car.
   - Frequent absences leading to truancy warnings.
   - Birthday movie outing and Halloween costume shopping.

3. **First Ambiguous Physical Contact**
   - Cuddling in bed with the accidental hand on the chest.

4. **Introduction to Substance Use**
   - Drinking vodka together at age 13.
   - First experience with whip-its and passing them mouth-to-mouth.
   - Inclusion of the mother in substance use.

5. **Weed Smoking and Concealing**
   - Making homemade bong, smoking weed in the master bedroom bathroom.
   - The mother’s reluctant involvement.

6. **Family Dynamics and Emotional Conflict**
   - Truth or Dare games.
   - Conflicts between narrator and mother, manipulation by the boyfriend.
   - The mother's misconceptions about narrator’s attitude.

7. **Mental Health and Decline**
   - Boyfriend's suicide attempt and overdose.
   - Diagnosis of schizophrenia and deterioration of mental state.
   
8. **Escalation to Paranoia and Drug Binge**
   - The house fire suspected to be arson for insurance money.
   - Boyfriend's paranoia, belief in conspiracies, and bizarre behavior.
   - The boyfriend’s claim of being a hermaphrodite and delusions about famous twins.

9. **Sexual Abuse Incident**
   - Night on the couch when the boyfriend sexually assaults the narrator.
   - The narrator's fear and silence.
   - Subsequent emotional turmoil and confession to the mother.

10. **Aftermath and Family Fallout**
    - Mother’s reaction to the confession, forcing the narrator to leave.
    - Grand jury hearing with the mother’s public outburst.
    - Being left at aunt’s house alone and feeling isolated.

### Character Map

- **Narrator (Main Subject)**
  - Starts as a vulnerable 12-year-old, later skips to high school.
  - Seeks friendship, struggles with family dynamics.
  - Victim of manipulation and abuse.
  - Emotionally conflicted but strong enough to eventually reveal abuse.

- **Boyfriend of Mom**
  - Initially friend and confidant to the narrator.
  - Manipulative: skews perspectives between mother and narrator.
  - Substance abuser; dips into paranoia and schizophrenia.
  - Sexual predator: crosses boundaries sexually and emotionally.
  - Exhibits profound mental health decline.

- **Mother**
  - Works full time initially, then stops.
  - Sometimes participates in substance use.
  - Conflicted relationship with narrator.
  - Overly trusting or manipulated by boyfriend.
  - Reacts harshly to abuse revelation; guilty and protective but complicated.

- **Younger Sister**
  - Mentioned briefly as part of family context.

- **Mitchel (Best Friend)**
  - Online friend from Atlanta during narrator's early exposure to the Internet.

- **Jen Corey**
  - Friend who partakes in weed smoking sessions.

- **Grandparents**
  - Supportive figure that rescues narrator during family conflict.
  
- **Andrew (Friend)**
  - Present during the narrator’s family eviction incident.

- **Aunt Julie and her husband**
  - Provide temporary shelter when narrator is sent away.

   Visual: Raw, tense, muted cries, camera focusing on narrator’s frozen expression and feet in lap.

### Transitional Moments

- **Moving into Red Sunset Park apartment**  
  Marks beginning of closer interaction with boyfriend under mother’s increasing work burden.

- **Beginning of regular skipping school**  
  Transition from innocence to risk and secrecy.

- **First substance use (drinking & whip-its)**  
  Shifts narrative to risky behavior bonding mechanism.

- **Inclusion of mother in substance use**  
  Blurring of boundaries, escalation of family dysfunction.

- **Boyfriend’s suicide attempt and diagnosis of schizophrenia**  
  Emotional and mental shift toward instability.

- **Mother stopping work; no more skipping school**  
  Restricts narrator’s freedom and alters relationship dynamics.

- **Escalation of paranoia and drug binge leading to fire**  
  Increases tension in environment, hinting at upcoming breakdown.

- **Sexual assault event**  
  Marked profound change in relationship and narrator’s emotional state.

- **Confession to mother and subsequent fallout**  
  Break in the facade; start of open conflict and family fracture.

- **Removal to grandparents; grand jury hearing**  
  Legal and emotional shift; increased isolation and trauma for narrator.

- **Being left at aunt’s house alone**  
  Physical and emotional separation climaxing narrator’s sense of abandonment.
