### Scene List

**1. Childhood Loneliness and Family Estrangement**  
- The narrator describes being a lonely, strange kid with no friends and feeling imprisoned for the first seventeen years.  
- Emotional turning point: Early feelings of isolation and alienation from family except a few loved ones. Family's harsh judgment and labeling contribute to this sense of estrangement.

**2. Relationships with Grandparents and Aunts**  
- Grandparents are described as good people who made sacrifices and put their kids first.  
- Aunts are hostile and judgmental, especially regarding the narrator’s presence and situation.  
- Emotional turning point: Feeling unwanted and used like a burden by family members, who openly resent her and treat her as a problem.

**3. Absence of Mother in Early Life**  
- Mother was rarely around during the narrator’s childhood.  
- The narrator calls grandmother "mom" and grandfather "dad."  
- Emotional turning point: Realization of missing motherhood and growing tension from aunts; feeling unwelcome even in the grandparents' home.

**4. Teenage Years and Conflict with Grandparents**  
- Around 12, conflicts start between narrator and grandmother, some with grandfather.  
- By 17, forced to leave grandparents' home.  
- Emotional turning point: Realizing she has to leave family home; mixture of relief and hurt.

**5. Living Independently and Personal Growth**  
- Leaving home at 17, struggling with motels and friends' couches, finally getting a place at 23.  
- Emotional turning point: Gaining independence and some control over life, mixed with continued family rejection.

**6. Relationship with Sister and Family Ties**  
- Mostly cut ties except grandparents and sister; recent distance from sister due to her behavior.  
- Emotional turning point: Continued complicated family relationships and partial isolation.

**7. Grandfather’s Prostate Cancer and Visit**  
- Learning about grandfather’s illness, upset, visit to hospital after surgery.  
- Grandfather recognizes and warmly greets narrator.  
- Emotional turning point: Deep emotional connection and hope for acceptance despite lifestyle differences.

**8. Relationship with Grandmother**  
- Grandmother more understanding, interested in narrator’s writing and book.  
- Emotional turning point: Small but significant approval and connection.

**9. Abusive Relationship with Mother’s Boyfriend (Chad)**  
- Mother’s boyfriend (Chad) slowly builds trust, becomes a close friend, then molests narrator.  
- Mother denies the abuse, blames narrator equally or more.  
- Emotional turning point: Betrayal, abuse, and profound trauma leading to enduring feelings of guilt and isolation.

**10. Mother's Neglect and Exploitation**  
- Mother blames narrator after abuse, acts psychotically, shows up at courthouse yelling accusations.  
- Emotional turning point: Loss of maternal support and overt hostility from mother.

**11. Repeated Abuse and Abandonment by Mother**  
- Mother "shoots up" narrator, asks her to engage in sexual acts, sends her out on the streets.  
- Emotional turning point: Crossing boundaries and total exploitation deepen trauma and mistrust.

**12. Forced Removal and Homelessness at 14**  
- Mother and stepfather relapse; narrator forced to leave home due to false accusations.  
- Emotional turning point: Feeling unwanted, burdened, and angry; desire for independence intensifies.

**13. Marriage to David and New Betrayal**  
- Marries David, later finds out he had slept with mother.  
- Mother feigns victimhood, manipulates to drive wedge between narrator and David.  
- Emotional turning point: Double betrayal—husband and mother—shattering trust and hope for love.

**14. Pregnancy and Mother’s Cold Advice**  
- First pregnancy at 17, wants abortion, calls mother for help; mother coldly tells her to turn tricks to afford it.  
- Emotional turning point: Crushing loneliness and lack of support in a vulnerable moment.

**15. Attempt to Reconnect with Mother**  
- Narrator tries to help mother with money, rides, and pretends everything is okay; mother exploits this without love or apology.  
- Emotional turning point: Realization that forgiveness and love from mother will never come.

**16. Continuing Abuse and Exploitation by Mother**  
- Mother crosses more boundaries involving drugs and sexual acts; narrator resents knowing mother’s true nature.  
- Emotional turning point: Deepened hatred and emotional withdrawal from mother.

**17. Returning to Grandparents’ Home with Mother Staying There**  
- Mother stays at grandparents' house despite her history; narrator feels betrayed by all family allowing this.  
- Emotional turning point: Sense of ultimate rejection by entire family; feeling alone and unsupported.

**18. Family Funeral and Missing Great-Grandmother**  
- Narrator visits great-grandmother's grave with grandfather on Mother's Day; reflects on her special nature and shared love for animals.  
- Emotional turning point: Nostalgic connection to family history and desire to embody positive qualities of ancestors.

**19. Emotional Exhaustion and Need for Expression**  
- Narrator concludes the story, acknowledging tiredness but stressing the need to articulate feelings about family, love, and betrayal.  
- Emotional turning point: Catharsis and resolve to express feelings openly despite exhaustion.
